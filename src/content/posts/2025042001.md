Human motion has two clocks. One ticks quickly: the bend of a knee, the turn of a shoulder, the small correction before a foot lands. The other moves more slowly: the cadence of walking, the arc of an action, and the intention that carries a pose into the next several seconds. **DAFCN**, short for *Dual-Path Attention Fourier Convolutional Network*, was built to listen to both.

## Why a dual path?

Many motion predictors are strongest at one horizon. Recurrent, MLP, and graph-based models can follow recent poses well, yet their forecasts may become smooth, delayed, or unstable when asked to look farther ahead. DAFCN separates the problem into two complementary streams:

- A **Motion Attention** path searches the observed sequence for the most useful short-term cues. It pays attention to the frames that best explain what the body is doing now.
- A **Fast Fourier Convolution** path works on broader temporal structure. By mixing information in the frequency domain, it is designed to retain periodic and long-range motion patterns that are easy to lose in a purely local model.

The two predictions meet in the output layers. The result is not a choice between reflex and rhythm, but a forecast informed by both.

## Reproducible from training to evaluation

The public implementation targets Human3.6M and keeps the workflow deliberately direct. After installing the Python requirements and a CUDA-compatible PyTorch build, the dataset is arranged by subject and the model can be trained with:

```bash
python train.py --data_dir path/to/dataset/Human3.6M
```

Evaluation uses the same data layout plus a saved checkpoint:

```bash
python test.py \
  --data_dir path/to/dataset/Human3.6M \
  --model_path checkpoint/train_in50_out10_ks10_dctn20
```

Keeping the commands small matters: a research repository is most useful when the path from paper idea to reproduced result is visible rather than hidden behind a large framework.

## Publication

The method is described in *A Dual-Path Attention Fourier Convolutional Network for Human Motion Prediction*, published in 2025 in the *International Journal of Current Research in Science, Engineering and Technology*. The repository contains the official implementation, architecture figure, data instructions, and citation metadata.
