On **October 4, 2026**, I released three new covers sung by **那由歌** on Bilibili: **二人三角**, **手を半分だけ**, and **依存症**. All three were written by **阿良々木健**, but they do not feel like repetitions of the same idea. Each song asks for a different visual temperature: burning sunset, nearly weightless white, and a pastel world that is beautiful enough to become unsettling.

For these releases, the PVs were built as three separate short stories. The final videos are presented in 2560 × 1440, with scene-specific illustrations, animated shots, synchronized Japanese subtitles, and title and credit cards assembled into a shared Premiere project. The images below are selected stills from those production archives; the videos themselves remain on Bilibili only.

## 二人三角 — a triangle drawn for two

![二人三角 PV still: 那由歌 against a burning sunset](/assets/posts/nayuta-futarisankaku.webp)

*二人三角* is the sharpest of the three. Its lyrics place forbidden affection, jealousy, prayer, and self-denial inside a relationship that can never become a simple line between two people. The PV answers that tension with a saturated orange-red sunset: silhouettes stay far apart, the horizon feels enormous, and even the warmest light seems capable of burning.

那由歌 stands inside that landscape without overpowering it. Her blue-white design cuts through the red sky, turning her into both narrator and witness. As the song moves forward, the setting shifts through school corridors, a riverside platform, and a night train—ordinary places made emotionally unstable by everything left unsaid.

[Watch 二人三角 on Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV11SH662EFM/)

## 手を半分だけ — reaching without disappearing

![手を半分だけ PV still: 那由歌 reaching forward through a field of white](/assets/posts/nayuta-tewohanbundake.webp)

The visual language of *手を半分だけ* is almost the opposite. White space, pencil lines, pale blue fragments, and unfinished drawings give the PV a fragile openness. The song begins with hands joining so that neither person has to remain alone, then follows two girls who meet through drawing and learn to treat creation as a promise.

The title suggests a deliberately incomplete gesture: not taking everything, but offering enough of oneself for another person to hold on to. That idea appears throughout the imagery—hands reaching across the frame, sketches passed between friends, an empty easel waiting to be filled, and fireworks reflected beside the river. The restraint makes the moments of connection feel brighter.

[Watch 手を半分だけ on Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1NSH66mEQV/)

## 依存症 — sweetness with a pulse underneath

![依存症 PV still: 那由歌 in a luminous pastel city](/assets/posts/nayuta-izonshou.webp)

*依存症* begins with the language of a love song—one voice, one wind chime, one person quietly watched—and gradually reveals how completely that affection has occupied the speaker's world. The PV embraces that contradiction. Its rooms and streets are soft, luminous, and almost medicinal: heart monitors, glass vessels, mirrors, flowers, and pink-blue light turn romance into both comfort and diagnosis.

That brightness is intentional. Instead of illustrating dependence as something visibly dark, the video lets it look inviting. The prettier the world becomes, the harder it is to tell whether it is a refuge or a closed system. 那由歌's calm presence anchors the shifting spaces while the song moves from tenderness toward fixation.

[Watch 依存症 on Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV11SH662EGM/)

## Three PVs, one release

Seen together, the three covers form a compact triptych about closeness. *二人三角* looks at desire complicated by a third point; *手を半分だけ* imagines connection as something carefully shared; *依存症* asks what happens when connection becomes the entire world. The color scripts move with those ideas—from fire, to paper and sky, to a glowing dream clinic—while 那由歌's voice remains the thread joining them.

All three videos were published within the same minute on October 4. That timing suits them: separate stories, presented side by side, meant to be heard as a set without losing their individual shapes.
