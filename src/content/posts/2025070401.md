A skeleton sequence looks simple—just joints moving through 3D coordinates—but those coordinates tell only one version of the story. As feature dimensions grow, a purely Euclidean representation can distort or discard relationships that matter to the motion itself. **CBFL**, or *Cross-space Behavior-aware Feature Learning*, approaches the sequence from two mathematical viewpoints at once.

## Two views of the same movement

CBFL is made of two complementary extractors:

1. The **Geometric Algebra Dependency-Aware Extractor** introduces geometric-algebra fully connected layers. It learns compact action dependencies in a space designed to express geometric relationships directly.
2. The **Euclidean Kinematic-Aware Extractor** stays close to familiar coordinates, using temporal Kinematic-Aware Attention and spatial Kinematic-Aware Feature Extraction to model when joints move and how the body is connected.

The value is in the conversation between them. Euclidean space preserves concrete kinematics; geometric algebra provides another lens on orientation and dependency. Their fused behavior-aware features help the model carry a motion forward without flattening its structure.

## What the experiments cover

The implementation includes experiment entry points for three widely used benchmarks:

- **Human3.6M**, for controlled indoor human actions;
- **AMASS / CMU MoCap**, for broader motion-capture sequences;
- **3DPW**, for motion recorded in real-world scenes.

Training and evaluation parameters are explicit in the repository. For example, Human3.6M training specifies the observation and prediction windows, DCT coefficients, input dimensions, and batch sizes in one command. Checkpoints can be evaluated by adding `--is_eval` and the checkpoint path, making it possible to follow the same pipeline from data preparation to reported metrics.

## Result and publication

The paper reports that CBFL improves existing methods by **4.3% MPJPE on average on Human3.6M**, supported by both quantitative and qualitative comparisons. The work appeared in *Scientific Reports* in 2025 as *Learning behavior aware features across spaces for improved 3D human motion prediction* (volume 15, article 28355).

The code release is archived separately on Zenodo as version v1, giving the implementation a citable snapshot in addition to the active GitHub repository.
