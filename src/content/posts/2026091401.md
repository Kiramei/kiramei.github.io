On **September 14, 2026**, I published a new 那由歌 cover on Bilibili: **ながれぼし**, written by ふわりP. At 6:46, it is the longest entry in this small cover series so far—and the one that leaves the most room for silence, distance, and the slow afterglow of a melody.

![那由歌 looking up at a shooting star](/assets/posts/nayuta-nagareboshi.webp)

## Another light from ふわりP's first album

*ながれぼし* means “shooting star.” The song first appeared as track seven on ふわりP's self-titled debut album in 2011. That same ten-track album also contains *またあした*, *ねがいごと*, and *きらり*—the three songs I covered previously.

That connection gives this release a lovely sense of continuity. It is not a separate detour, but another point in the same constellation: a song that has always been close to the others, waiting for its turn to be heard in 那由歌's voice.

## A song that refuses to hurry

The arrangement unfolds patiently. Rather than pushing toward an immediate climax, it allows each phrase to rise and disappear like light moving through a wide night sky. The gentle pacing is essential to its character: warmth arrives gradually, and the pauses feel just as meaningful as the sung notes.

The cover keeps that openness intact. 那由歌's softer lines remain delicate, while the brighter passages expand naturally instead of becoming theatrical. The result feels intimate and observant—as if the singer is not trying to catch the shooting star, only standing still long enough to see it clearly.

## The fourth page of a small cover journal

The earlier covers formed a subtle emotional sequence. *またあした* carried a tender farewell; *ねがいごと* held a wish close to the heart; *きらり* turned toward a small, unmistakable sparkle. *ながれぼし* now sends that sparkle across the whole sky.

It is a fleeting image, but not an empty one. A shooting star disappears almost as soon as it is noticed, yet the wish made beneath it remains. That is where this cover feels most at home: between memory and tomorrow, letting one brief line of light connect the two.
